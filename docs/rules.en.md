## Rules of play

Salta is played by two opponents on a Salta board of 100 squares; one uses the green pieces, the other the 15 red ones. The whole game moves on the black squares only. Set up, the board looks like this:

So the suns stand in row I, the moons in row II, and the stars in row III, the side facing the player; each of the two opponents has their pieces before them in the order 1 to 5 from the left, beginning with row I.

The game consists in both players having to bring their pieces to the opposite side, and in such a way that they finally stand in the same order of battle as at the start.\* Both opponents move turn by turn, always from one square to one of the four neighbouring squares, provided it is empty; but as soon as a neighbouring square is occupied by an opponent's piece, that piece must be jumped, provided the next square in the same line is free.

## Moves

1. I move a piece as in draughts onto a neighbouring black square, either diagonally forwards or diagonally backwards.

2. If an opponent's piece comes to stand diagonally in front of one of mine, then, the following square being free, I must jump the opponent's piece, as in draughts. No piece is ever removed, however.

3. Should I forget to jump, my opponent must call out "Salta" (jump!) to me.

4. So I may not push a piece somewhere else when I have a jump to make anywhere on the board. **Making double or triple jumps at once is not permitted. Jumping backwards does not count either**, although, as noted above, **moving backwards** does.

5. If I am put in a jumping position at **several** places at once, I may jump at **one** place only; my opponent must then make a move before I may carry out the further jumps, for the whole game consists of nothing but alternating moves, or jumps, by the two opponents.

Once I (A) have led all my pieces to their correct destination (see Fig. 2), my opponent (B) has lost, and by as many Points as the moves they would still need on their own to have all their pieces at their destination too (in Fig. 2 that is ten moves, so 10 Points lost for B and won by A).

**The art of Salta consists chiefly in three things: first, in bringing one's own pieces nearer to their destinations; second, in wresting good positions from the enemy, which is achieved above all by breaking up the opposing troops; and third, in the conquest of as much terrain as possible.**

The number of fighting methods that may be employed to this end is quite considerable, and their combinations yield the most varied styles of play: the gambit opening, the boring method, the covered diagonal lines, cover, the jump lid, the sealing wall, retirement, contraction, pinning down, and so on.†

The more Salta one plays, the more these notions come to mind.

> † Compare *Salta, das neue Brettspiel* by Prof. Dr. Schubert.

## Tournament rule

In tournament play both opponents play only 120 moves each. If the game is not over by then, **each** player moves whichever of their pieces are not yet in order to their destination. From that point the enemy pieces no longer count as an obstacle. If, for example, green still has 10 moves to make and red 5, 6 or 9, then green has lost by 5, 4 or 1 Points respectively. The player moving second also has one move to make up at the end.

If both opponents have equally many Points to make up at the end, or if both come out at once, the game is undecided, or drawn.
